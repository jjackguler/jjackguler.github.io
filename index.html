08 — Leaving the cave

Out here, the work is not the shadows. It's the fire, and who holds it.

A proposal · in daylight

How I'd run AI enablement at CBC/Radio-Canada

A disciplined pipeline, a shared registry, and people who can see what is moving — built with content and operations leaders, not for them.

This is my proposal as a candidate — not CBC/Radio-Canada policy. It aligns with the public principles already in place: human accountability, transparency and disclosure, trust.

01 · Pipeline

From idea to rollout — with a human gate at every stage

  1. Intake

    One front door for every team, in English and French.

  2. Assess

    Value, risk, effort, editorial sensitivity.

  3. Experiment

    A baseline metric and an exit criterion — before anything is built.

  4. MVP incubation

    Small and real, owned by the team that will use it.

  5. Rollout

    Training with L&D, a playbook, support.

  6. Measure & share

    Results go back to the registry, the dashboard and case studies.

StageGate — human editorial / legal / security sign-off where relevant
02 · First 90 days — my proposal

Listen, then build the frame, then show visible work

30days

Listen and map

  • A shared registry of the AI tools and pilots already in use across English and French services.
  • Meet content, operations, legal, security, People & Culture and L&D leaders.
60days

One frame

  • One intake and assessment framework: value, risk, effort, editorial sensitivity.
  • A baseline metric and exit criterion for every pilot.
  • A lightweight responsible-AI playbook aligned with existing CBC/Radio-Canada principles — human accountability, disclosure.
90days

Visible work

  • Two or three visible pilots chosen with content and operations leaders — not for them.
  • AI-literacy sessions co-built with L&D.
  • A pipeline dashboard — intake → experiment → MVP → rollout — so leadership can see what's moving.
03 · Principles

Trust is the most precious asset

  • Human accountabilityNo journalism without direct human involvement. A named person answers for every AI-assisted result.
  • DisclosureAudiences are told when and how AI is used. Transparency is part of the product.
  • Bilingual by defaultTools, training and playbooks built for English and French services from day one.
  • TrustEvery pilot is judged against it — with legal, security and governance at the table, and collaboration over competition.
